Prototyping a focused product page where the key specs, the variants and the buy button are all visible at once.

Overview

Everything needed to decide, in one view.

This is a concept product page for the REDMAGIC 9 Pro, a gaming phone. The aim was a page where a shopper can see the phone, understand what makes it different and choose a configuration without scrolling or hunting.

I designed the page and prototyped its interactions in Figma — flipping through product images, switching between variants and moving toward purchase.

The problem

What was getting in the way

  1. Specs get buriedThe details that sell a gaming phone are often pushed below long marketing sections.
  2. Variant choice is easy to missColour and storage options are frequently hidden in dropdowns.
  3. Too many steps to buyA shopper who has decided shouldn’t have to search for the button.
Approach

Designed around a purchase decision

The page follows the order a shopper makes up their mind in.

  1. 01SeeWhat does it look like?
  2. 02ScanWhat makes it different?
  3. 03ChooseWhich colour and storage?
  4. 04BuyHow do I get it?
Key decisions

Why it works the way it does

Decision 01

Everything above the fold

The product image, five key specs, the price, the variants and both actions sit together in the first view.

Decision 02

Variants as visible choices

Sleet, Cyclone and Snowfall appear as selectable buttons with their memory and storage, so the options are compared at a glance.

Decision 03

A clear action hierarchy

A solid red Buy Now leads, with an outlined Add to cart beside it for shoppers who aren’t ready.

Screens

The prototype

The product page, with image carousel, variant selection and purchase actions.

Outcome

What changed

  • A product page that fits the whole purchase decision into one view
  • Variants presented as visible, comparable choices
  • A clickable Figma prototype of the core interactions
What I learned

Building this as a prototype rather than a static screen made me think about states and transitions — what changes when a variant is picked — not just how the page looks at rest.
