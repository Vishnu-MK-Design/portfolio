Play Anime

Designing an anime streaming site that puts discovery first — what’s ranked, what’s popular and what’s coming soon.

Overview

A home for anime fans that starts with discovery.

Play Anime is a site for watching anime in the highest possible quality. It carries exclusive titles, and it lets fans find out which new anime are coming and when they will be released.

I designed the brand mark, the visual system and the homepage — choosing type and colour that let the artwork carry the page, and structuring the homepage into rows that each answer a question a fan brings with them.

The problem

What was getting in the way

  1. A huge catalogue is hard to browseFans need a way in that doesn’t start with scrolling an endless grid.
  2. New releases get lostUpcoming anime and premiere dates are exactly what fans look for, and are easy to bury.
  3. Artwork competes with the interfaceAnime key art is loud and colourful, so the UI has to step back without disappearing.
  4. Watching should be one step awayFrom any title, starting to watch needed to be obvious.
Approach

Designed around how fans browse

The homepage reads top to bottom as a sequence of questions, each answered by its own row.

  1. 01FeatureWhat is big right now?
  2. 02SearchCan I find a title fast?
  3. 03RankedWhat are people watching?
  4. 04UpcomingWhat is releasing next?
  5. 05WatchHow do I start?
Key decisions

Why it works the way it does

Decision 01

Let the artwork lead

A full-width hero and a near-black #121212 ground let the key art bring the colour, so every title looks its best.

Decision 02

One colour for action

The amber-to-orange accent is kept for actions — start watching, add to list, log in — so the next step always stands out from the artwork.

Decision 03

Rows by intent

Top ranked, most popular, upcoming, recommended and genres each get their own row, with featured titles breaking up the rhythm.

Outcome

What changed

  • A recognisable brand mark and visual identity for the site
  • A homepage structured as discovery rows rather than a single grid
  • A dedicated section for upcoming anime and release dates
  • A dark, artwork-first visual system with a single colour for action
What I learned

Designing around loud, colourful artwork taught me how much restraint an interface needs. The quieter the UI, the more the content — and the one colour reserved for action — could do.
